#+TITLE: 4 · Model checking over the IR
#+STARTUP: showall

* Seeing through a call

The model checker proves properties of a stateful process by translating its
guards and transitions into SMT formulas a solver can decide. That translator
used to read the /surface/ syntax tree, which has one blind spot: a call. A
guard written as =(safe? balance)=, where =safe?= is a helper, is opaque to a
surface translator — it becomes =:untranslatable=, and the transition it guards
is silently dropped. A proof that quietly ignores a transition is worse than no
proof.

Translating the IR fixes this. Operators arrive already resolved, and a call is
a clear node whose callee is known. So when the callee is *proven pure*
(=system.analyze= footprint = ∅), the translator can look up its body and inline
it — the formula then models the helper's actual computation.

* The surface translator stops at the call; the IR translator inlines it

#+begin_src beam-lisp :id inline
(ns demo (:require [anf-smt :as as] [system.smt :as smt]))
(def prog "(defn margin [b r] (erlang/- b r))\n(defn safe? [b] (erlang/>= b 0))")
(println (pr-str
  {:surface-sees (smt/of-source #{"balance"} "(safe? balance)")
   :anf-inlines  (as/of-source prog ["balance"] "(safe? balance)")
   :nested       (as/of-source prog ["balance" "reserved"]
                               "(erlang/>= (margin balance reserved) 0)")}))
#+end_src

#+RESULTS: inline
: {:surface-sees :untranslatable, :anf-inlines (>= balance 0), :nested (>= (- balance reserved) 0)}
: :ok

The surface layer returns =:untranslatable= for the helper call; the IR
translator inlines =safe?= to =(>= balance 0)=, and the nested =(margin b r)= to
=(- b r)=.

* An invariant proven through a helper

A server whose safety margin is a pure helper — invariant =margin(b,r) ≥ 0=,
withdrawal guarded by =amt ≤ margin(b,r)= — is now provable end to end:

#+begin_src beam-lisp :id prove
(ns demo (:require [anf-smt :as as] [z3]))
(def prog "(defn margin [b r] (erlang/- b r))")
(def inv      (as/of-source prog ["balance" "reserved"] "(erlang/>= (margin balance reserved) 0)"))
(def inv-post (as/of-source prog ["bpost" "reserved"]   "(erlang/>= (margin bpost reserved) 0)"))
(def guard    (as/of-source prog ["balance" "reserved" "amt"] "(erlang/=< amt (margin balance reserved))"))
(def port (z3/open))
(defn obligation [with-guard]
  (z3/check port
    (concat
      (list (list 'declare-const 'balance 'Int) (list 'declare-const 'reserved 'Int)
            (list 'declare-const 'amt 'Int) (list 'declare-const 'bpost 'Int)
            (list 'assert inv) (list 'assert (list '>= 'amt 0)))
      (if with-guard (list (list 'assert guard)) (list))
      (list (list 'assert (list '= 'bpost (list '- 'balance 'amt)))
            (list 'assert (list 'not inv-post))))))
(println (pr-str {:with-guard (obligation true) :without-guard (obligation false)}))
#+end_src

#+RESULTS: prove
: {:with-guard "unsat", :without-guard "sat"}
: :ok

=unsat= with the guard means the invariant is preserved through the helper;
=sat= without it means the solver finds a counterexample. The proof is real, and
it passes through the abstraction the surface translator could not.

* Why the inline is sound

The inline is gated on the footprint proof. A pure helper has no effects to
model, so substituting its body into a formula changes nothing about the world —
it is a pure value computation, exactly what SMT reasons about. An impure helper
is correctly *not* inlined; it stays =:untranslatable=, honestly. The same
=footprint = ∅= fact that lets a function go native (page 5) lets its callers be
proven here — one proof, two payoffs.
