* Corrected 2026-09-18 (this item was filed as "defqueue" and the user pushed
  back; the push-back was right, and the doc agrees with itself)

**A `defqueue` form is the wrong move.** The doc prescribes it in §4.3 as it was
first written, and contradicts itself two ways:

  * §4.1.1: *"`defbeat` is deleted"* — the tick form existed, the `(tick …)`
    clause landed, and the FORM was removed, because *"a macro is a fork"* while
    a clause's `:init` *"is what makes a clause COMPOSABLE"*.
  * §3.2: *"a relation + a tick + a watch"*, with the durable patterns —
    *Idempotency Key* and *Claim/Lease* — named as needing **no new machinery**.

A queue is therefore the store you already have, plus a claim loop, plus the
clause that declares both. `priv/std/proc/server.bl` says as much in its header:
*"one `defserver`, and an OPEN vocabulary of clauses"* — a clause is a
**registration**, so a new capability costs a registration, not a form.

* The shape (docs/scheduling/the-clock-and-the-wheel.md §4.3, corrected)

```clojure
(defserver larder
  ;; the store IS the queue; the clause is the hand that turns it
  (queue {:of          :hotel/order
          :concurrency 4
          :retry       {:max 5 :backoff :exponential :base 1000 :max 60000 :jitter 0.2}
          :unique      [:order/room :order/item]   ; the STORE's identity
          :lease       300
          :partition   [:order/room]})
  (perform [job]
    (charge! job)
    (assoc job :status :done)))
```

and the enqueue is a **write**, because a job must be a fact before it can be
owed:

```clojure
(call :larder [! :enqueue {:room "101" :item :breakfast}])
(datom/q (proc/conn :larder) '[:find ?room ?status :where …])   ; the pane asks no one
```

* What is actually new (everything else is shipped)

  1. **the claim loop** — `priv/std/proc/queue.bl`, a clause registered like
     `(tick …)` and `(sched …)`, registered as `:bl-queue/<server>` by its own
     init. It reads due work from the store and runs it.
  2. **the claim transaction** — take-and-mark in one guarded transact. This is
     the piece to PROVE (BUG-022 already shows concurrent `transact!` rewinding a
     basis), and it is the whole of *Claim/Lease*.
  3. **at-least-once, said out loud** — the `perform` contract: a body may run
     twice after a crash, so it must be idempotent or declared otherwise. A queue
     that does not say this lies by omission.
  4. **the eligibility rules on `enqueue!`** — uniqueness (via the store's
     identity, not a key table) and a delayed/`:at` time.
  5. **`stats`** — a query over the store, not a counter.

  NOT new, and must not be re-built: durability (the store), retry bookkeeping
  (columns + the claim loop's clock), pause/resume (`proc/op!` — the sched
  precedent), the pane's read path (the store), prune (**a `:schedules`
  declaration** of the tree that owns the queue — P4's machinery, which is
  precisely what `:schedules` was built to be able to say), and any registry of
  queues (the name is derived from the declaration, as `:bl-sched/<server>` is).

* Why the clause and not the form — the two costs, measured elsewhere

  * **composability**: a worker will want a `handle` for its own backlog, a
    `tick` for a sweep, a `sched` for the prune. A form cannot carry them; a
    clause's contributions are injected *before* the server's own, so they
    compose. `defbeat` is the measured precedent.
  * **declaration vs registration**: `(perform [job] …)` inside the form is
    declared where the queue is; the form-free version needs
    `(jobs/perform q :kind (fn …))` — a runtime registration, i.e. two places for
    one fact.

* Acceptance

  * a job enqueued and a daemon killed before it runs still runs after restart —
    and a job that already ran does NOT run again (the `a-restart-resumes-it-does-not-repeat`
    precedent from P2b).
  * concurrency never exceeds `:concurrency` (the invariant is declarable and
    checkable, `system/verify`).
  * a failing `perform` retries with bounded backoff, records attempts, and
    `:failed` is a VISIBLE state — not a lost job.
  * two enqueues of the same unique key inside the window produce ONE job.
  * the pane's `:jobs` section renders from the store with no process asked, and
    `prune` runs as a `:schedules` declaration.
  * knowledger's `run-job-async!` + `runner-sup` + `supervised-job` + `job-worker`
    delete down to `enqueue` + `perform` over the control-space store it already
    uses for `:job/*`.
