* Four classes, and only two of them are "load-bearing Elixir" in the bad sense

1. IRREDUCIBLE SUBSTRATE (keep, and say why): the Core Erlang emitter and linker
   (`emit.ex`, `link.ex`, `compiler_data.ex`, `atom_guard.ex`, `record.ex`), the
   NIF bridge (`native.ex`), the port-level machinery (`z3_port.ex`,
   `daemon/*.ex` — sockets, framing, group leaders, process supervision). These
   are the ERTS/Elixir floor PLAN-108's thesis names: Core Erlang → BEAM and the
   BEAM's own process/socket model. Moving them is not a gap, it is the floor.

2. DEAD OR DUPLICATED, and therefore the CHEAPEST moves: delete, do not port.
   * `model.ex` (160 lines) — the embedding's destination rules. `embed-asset.bl`
     now owns them for the writer, and `code/embed.bl` (beam-lisp!) still asks
     `BeamLisp.Model` for the reader's answer, so the same rules exist twice.
     The move is: put `dir/1`, `files/0`, `searched_dirs/1` in the LIBRARY tier
     (where the reader lives — driver→library requires are the allowed
     direction), point both callers at it, delete `model.ex` and
     `test/beam_lisp/model_test.exs`. ~160 lines gone, one truth.
   * `drop.ex` — an Elixir facade over the beam-lisp drop codec in `priv/build/`.
     Check its callers first; if `bl` reaches the codec directly, the facade is
     a second door.
   * `build_task.ex` / `native_task.ex` — Elixir shells whose behaviour is in
     `priv/build/*.bl`. `BuildTask` is called by eleven test files (ExUnit is
     Elixir, so a shell must exist) but its *rules* are already beam-lisp.

3. RUNTIME PRIMITIVES (the big prize, and the big risk): `rt.ex` (2,928) and
   `env.ex` (1,374) are the language's own machinery. A Lisp implementing its
   own runtime in itself is exactly the end state, and exactly the thing that
   must not be attempted without the verification substrate (PLAN-092 LazySeq
   ownership, PLAN-094/095 the ANF engine + types) standing behind it.

4. DEV-ONLY (deprioritise): `dev_change.ex`, `dev_server.ex`, and the reload
   plumbing. They do not ship in a drop and nothing a user runs depends on them.

* Acceptance for this item

  Each entry above is either DELETED (with the reason it was dead or duplicated)
  or MOVED (with the rules living in one beam-lisp namespace both callers
  require). No entry is "moved" by copying the logic to `.bl` and leaving the
  Elixir in place — that is how `model.ex` and `embed-asset.bl` disagree today,
  and disagreement is worse than either implementation alone.
