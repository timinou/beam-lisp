This is a **research spike with a written recommendation as its deliverable**,

* The question

Specter sits at **4 of 31 behaving** (23 of 31 loading). The load/behave gap is
the finding: shipping the syntax Specter needs (=defn-=, =reify=, =#?=,
=defprotocol= docstrings, =deftype=/=defrecord=, =identical?=) doubled the load
score and moved behaviour by three. What remains is **Specter's own
implementation engine**, not beam-lisp grammar:

- =i/NONE= sentinel (prerequisite of 5 slices' genuine behaviour)
- the =path= macro + the =i/compiled-*= family (slices 18-21)
- =i/comp-paths*= (22)
- =i/exec-select*= / =i/exec-transform*= expansion (13, 28)
- =mutable-cell= / =compiled-traverse*= (30)
- =i/srange-transform*= (11, 14)
- =doseqres= (07)

The entry-point macros state the problem in miniature: =select=, =select-any=,
=transform=, =setval= **all load** — they compile their backquote templates
without resolving =path=/=i/compiled-*= — and **none can run**, because their
expansion *is* the compiled-path caching machinery. Macro-heavy libraries load
far more readily than they run.

* Why gated on wave 27

Three hard reasons, in order of severity.

1. **The measurement is currently untrustworthy at exactly this boundary.**
   Until wave 27's prims land, ~10 of the 19 load-only slices cannot even be
   *behaviour-tested* — they die on a missing =type=/=vec=/=subs=/=dissoc=
   before reaching any engine code. Starting the spike now would profile the
   prim gap and mistake it for the engine.

2. **The macro stack is still walled off.** Wave 27's A1 (quoted-symbol
   map-destructure key) unblocks =nav=/=richnav= and therefore
   =defnav=/=defrichnav=. Until then the *navigator construction* half of
   Specter is unreachable, so any engine design would be drawn against an
   incomplete picture of what the engine must accept.

3. **This project has already been burned by exactly this.** =reify= was
   ranked as unlocking 6 navigator slices, was built, and unlocked **zero** —
   the real blocker was one level earlier. A spike into the engine before the
   cheaper walls fall is the same mistake with a much larger budget.

* Deliverable

A written recommendation in =docs/specter-compat.md= (new section) that
answers, with evidence:

** 1. What the engine actually requires of the host language

Read =/tmp/specter= (=impl.cljc= 1004 lines, =navs.cljc= 786) and enumerate the
host capabilities the compiled-path machinery assumes. Known candidates, to be
confirmed or refuted by reading:

- mutable cells (=:clj= branch uses a Java class =com.rpl.specter.MutableCell=)
- identity-based caching of compiled paths
- =:inline= metadata / compile-time path resolution
- protocol dispatch performance characteristics
- JVM-specific interop in the =:clj= branches

For each: does the BEAM have an equivalent, a workable analogue, or nothing?
A mutable cell is trivial on the BEAM (=:atomics=, process dictionary, ETS,
or beam-lisp's existing =volatile!=) — the question is whether Specter's
*semantics* survive the substitution.

** 2. Which of the three routes to recommend

- **Implement faithfully.** Port =i/comp-paths*= and the compiled-path cache.
  Highest fidelity; largest cost. Estimate it in slices-unlocked per unit of
  work, and say what it would NOT unlock.
- **Approximate.** Provide a beam-lisp-native navigator engine that satisfies
  the same *user-facing* API (=select=/=transform=/=setval=) without Specter's
  internal caching design. Cheaper; would move the behave number; but the
  vendored fixtures call *internal* fns, so it may move the API without moving
  the *measurement*. Be explicit about that distinction — it is the trap.
- **Decline, and say so.** Record Specter as measured-and-bounded: "beam-lisp
  reaches 4/31 on Specter; the remainder is Specter's engine, which is a
  port project in its own right." Then point users at =priv/optics.bl=, which
  already provides lenses and traversals natively and needed no compiler
  changes.

** 3. The honest cost/benefit

Specter is a **measuring stick**, not a dependency. The project already ships
its own optics library. So the spike must answer: *what does raising the
Specter number buy, beyond the number?* Plausible real answers — it would
prove beam-lisp can host a genuinely hard macro-heavy library; it would likely
surface a further class of latent bugs the way every prior measurement did.
Plausible negative answer — it is a large port of someone else's internals
that teaches less per unit of work than a fourth jank widening or a new
measured target.

Name a **cheaper alternative target** if one exists and would teach more
(candidates worth weighing: =core.match=, =clojure.spec=, =medley=,
=meander=). "Measure something else instead" is a legitimate recommendation.

NOTE [2026-08-09]: UNBLOCKED. PLAN-016 (wave 27) is DONE, and the gate it was waiting on has

WHY THIS WAS BLOCKED, AND WHAT CHANGED
The item was held because the measurement could not be trusted at exactly
this boundary: ~10 of the 19 load-only slices died on a missing prim before
reaching any engine code, so a spike would have profiled the prim gap and
called it the engine. That is no longer true.

Wave 27 shipped every prim on the list, plus four gaps the measurement
itself surfaced (`for`, `declare`, variadic `vary-meta`, `^Tag` bindings)
and two compiler bugs (macro-attached metadata; syntax-quote not
namespace-qualifying). Specter moved load 23 -> 25 and behave 4 -> 9.

THE SPIKE NOW HAS THE PRECONDITION IT NEEDED
The canonical cross-namespace navigator stack LOADS: slices 01, 02, 03, 04,
05, 23, 24. Slices 15 and 26 stop inside `i/direct-nav-obj` — Specter's own
internals. `docs/specter-compat.md` now states plainly that **there are no
known beam-lisp gaps left in this target**. Every remaining failure is
Specter's impl machinery, a non-vendored upstream dep, or
correct-by-construction (29 has no `:clj` branch and SHOULD fail to read).

That is precisely the condition under which the spike's question becomes
answerable: whatever the engine work costs, no amount of *language* work
will move this number further. The distance is now purely library-shaped.

WHAT TO CARRY IN
- The prediction loop keeps paying. This round: `for`/`vary-meta` were
  correctly named as the defnav blockers and delivered 23/24, but 15/26 did
  not follow because their real blocker was one level deeper. `^Tag`
  materialised at EXPANSION only — the macros expand, then behaviour fails
  at `.select*` host dispatch. Record half-materialised predictions as such.
- Wave 27 is also evidence FOR the "decline" option being respectable. The
  most valuable things it produced were not Specter slices at all: a compiler
  bug four slices shared, a syntax-quote gap that constrained every beam-lisp
  library shipping a macro, and the discovery that `for` did not exist. If a
  cheaper target (core.match, clojure.spec, medley, meander) would surface
  that class of finding at lower cost, saying so is the right answer.

The deliverable is unchanged: a written recommendation — implement,
approximate, or decline — with the host-capability enumeration and a
slices-unlocked estimate per route. Declining, and recording Specter as
measured-and-bounded, remains fully acceptable.
