* What is wanted

The clause vocabulary (`server.bl`: *"one `defserver`, and an OPEN vocabulary of
clauses"*) currently lives **in std**, and the doc says exactly why that is a rung
and not the summit (§4.1.1):

> *the clause therefore lands first as a std macro over [`defgen`], and is
> replaced by the real clause when the ladder climbs. That is a cutover, not a
> parallel implementation.*

So the ladder is:

    boot    the special form, renamed `defgen` — the generation machine
    std     `defserver` as a macro over it (what exists today)
    →       the ladder climbs, the macro is replaced by the real thing

and the piece the doc calls *"the subtle half"*:

> **The generation counter.** `cancel_timer` cannot un-send a […] so a message
> from a previous generation must be REFUSED, or a canceled clause runs anyway.

* Why it is not merely a rename

  * **`cancel_timer` cannot un-send.** Any clause whose body was already in
    flight (timer fired, message queued) is a *stale generation*'s work. Without
    the counter it runs, and the failure is invisible: an old `tick` body writing
    a slice that no longer exists, a canceled schedule firing once more.
  * **an installed DROP is a stale generation**, and that is not a metaphor: the
    doc's own line — *"installed DROP is a stale generation whose
    `priv/build/source-graph.bl` has no …"*. The same counter answers both the
    reload case and the image-drift case (FUP-084).
  * **the launcher is part of the blocker, and that was measured this session:**
    `./bl` (the ELF at the tree root) runs the DROP's prebuilt snapshot —
    `priv/std/bl/*` is committed as `.beam` there, so `bl.env`, `bl.cache` and
    `bl.cli` edits are INVISIBLE under `./bl`, while `priv/std/vm/*` (not in the
    drop) loads from source. `./bin/bl` runs the checkout's `build/`. That
    half-and-half is what made the ladder look blocked: the stale half was the
    drop, not the source. **All verification of this item goes through
    `./bin/bl`.**

* What exists now

  * `priv/std/server.bl` — the clause registry, transforms, `:prelude`/`:init`
    injection, real clauses (`priv/std/tick.bl`, `priv/std/proc/sched.bl`).
  * `bl build`'s `:ex` stage (`env.bl` `:build {:ex {:root "lib"}}`) — the rung
    that compiles the Elixir side, so "the Elixir half must be rebuilt" is a
    command, not a wish.
  * the drop/checkout split above, and FUP-084 (route daemon image drift through
    the reloader).

* Acceptance

  * `defserver` is DEFINED ONCE, in std, over the boot form — no second expansion
    path, no parallel implementation.
  * a stale generation's message is refused, with a test that fires a clause,
    replaces its generation, and asserts the old body does not run.
  * `defbeat`'s precedent holds: if the ladder makes a form redundant, the form
    is deleted (as `defbeat` was).
  * the daemon/image drift is visible as a fact (FUP-084), so "stale generation"
    is a readable state and not a mystery.
  * the working launcher is stated in docs/dev: `./bin/bl` for the checkout,
    `./bl` for the shipped drop — and a reader can tell which they are running.
