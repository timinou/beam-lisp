** Why these were deferred (honest reasons)

The live cutover flips the LOAD-BEARING `Compiler.compile/2` and deletes ~2400
lines. Its validation gate is the full `mix test` (863 tests), which:
- has a PRE-EXISTING Env-lifecycle flake (FUP-014) that makes pass/fail hard to
  read cleanly;
- takes ~15 min per run;
- a latent .bl bug on any runtime edge-case the AST-corpus (932/932) did not
  exercise would break the ability to compile ANYTHING, including the rebuild.
Rushing this at the end of a very long session is the one place where haste is
genuinely dangerous. The mechanism is de-risked; the careful wiring + full-suite
validation is what remains.

*** P10 — runtime prims to .bl (SUBSTRATE — benchmark-exempt by the floor rule)
- The 335 rt prims (first/rest/count/map/reduce/...) are the HOTTEST path: the
  compiler itself calls `first` 140x. They are already leaf ops over :erlang
  BIFs; porting to .bl and AOT-linking yields the SAME BEAM code but adds a
  bootstrap-ordering hazard (they must AOT before the compiler that uses them).
  By PLAN-057's own floor principle ("substrate migrates only if .bl-expressible
  WITHOUT a benchmarked perf loss"), they stay UNTIL a benchmark earns the move.
  The cutover does NOT touch them. If pursued: port a pure non-hot slice first
  (inc/dec/second/nth-derived), bench each op, keep any that regress as thin
  :erlang wrappers "earned by a number".
