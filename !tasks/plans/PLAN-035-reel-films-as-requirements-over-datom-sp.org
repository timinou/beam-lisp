* The thesis

A hotelcomm FILM (`test/e2e/film/scenarios/*.spec.js`) is not a test. It is an
executable narrative that says what the product must be able to show, and its
assertion messages carry the reason:

    "the guest's fault must have become an OPEN request row on the board — a
     reply with no work behind it is the failure this film exists to catch"

That sentence is a REQUIREMENT. The film is where requirements come from; the
board is what follows. So the tool's spine is:

    Film ──has──▶ Beat        (ordinal, label, narration, action)
    Film ──asserts──▶ Requirement  (statement + why it matters)
    Requirement ──needs──▶ Component  (surface | seam | capability | fixture)
    Component ──realised by──▶ Task
    Task ──owned by──▶ Lead  (tech | product | sales)

∴ a task with no requirement behind it is busywork; a requirement with no film
behind it is an opinion. The tool makes both visible as QUERIES, not as
discipline.

The three leads are not three apps. They are three questions over one graph:

  | lead | the question | the query |
  |---|---|---|
  | tech | what is not built? | components with no realising task, or tasks open |
  | product | what is not proven? | requirements no film asserts, films with no beats |
  | sales | what can I show? | films whose every requirement is satisfied |

* Why datom rather than a table

Three properties are load-bearing rather than decorative:

  1. `as-of` — "the board as it stood at the demo on Tuesday" is a filter over
     data that never went anywhere; no audit table, no snapshot job.
  2. `changes` — a task's lifecycle history IS the fact log; the activity feed
     is a read, not a written-alongside record that can disagree.
  3. datalog — "which requirements have no component" is one query, not a join
     someone remembered to write.

Durability is redb (`datom.store-redb/open` + `datom/connect-with`), verified
to recover data, schema, basis AND history across a process restart
(`examples/datom/06-terms-and-disk.bl`).

* The one upstream change: `do!`

`spell.server/walk` is a CLOSED interpreter: special forms plus a whitelist of
~30 pure functions, and an unknown head is refused by name. That is correct and
must stay — a walker that falls back to `apply` is an evaluator wearing a
costume — but it means no handler body can create a task.

The existing answer to exactly this problem is `ask!`: the walk RECORDS the
request and the caller performs it, so the interpreter keeps zero ambient
authority. `do!` is the same move, generalised:

    (on :create-task [title lead]
      (if (blank? title)
        (err "A task needs a title")
        (do (do! :create-task {:title title :lead lead})
            (ok "created"))))

  - `spell.server` records `{:op :create-task :payload {…}}` in the walk state
  - `BeamLisp.Spell.Server` performs each recorded intent, in order, through a
    REGISTERED performer, and merges the assigns it answers with

The performer is a beam-lisp fn (`reel.intent/perform`), so the domain logic
stays in `.bl`. Nothing about the walker's authority changes: it still only
records.
