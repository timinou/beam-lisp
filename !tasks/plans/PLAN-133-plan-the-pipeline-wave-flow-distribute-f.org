* Goal

P6 of `PLAN-126` — the Broadway half — as **a clause on `defserver`**, not a
`data defpipeline` form. The user asked the right question: `defpipeline` is the

**Wave A (running).** `flow/distribute` + `flow/batch` — the primitives, in
`priv/std/proc/flow.bl`. `flow` today has `from-seq`, `run` and a demand window,
and NO fan-out and NO batcher (verified). Its invariants are the whole game:
every event to exactly ONE worker (no loss, no duplication), the upstream window
is the SUM of the workers' demands (so a slow worker throttles the producer),
and a worker's death is reported, never swallowed. `batch` emits on N events or
T ms, whichever first.

**Wave B.** `priv/std/proc/pipeline.bl` — the clause.

```clojure
(proc/defserver kitchen
  (pipeline
    (from (orders))                       ; a source: a bus, a conn, a seq
    (stage :check desk/check {:concurrency 2})
    (batch :pan    8         {:timeout 150})
    (stage :cook  desk/cook  {:concurrency 4})
    (stage :plate desk/plate)
    (on-lag :block)                       ; or :drop — the policy is NAMED
    (on-error :requeue)))
```

One clause whose ENTRIES are validated forms — the precedent is `(sched (every
15 :min …) (daily 7 0 …))`, already shipped. An unknown entry is an error that
names the whole accepted set. It contributes `:prelude` (arming the topology at
server start) and `:init` (counters + the worker set); the demand and lag
handlers ride the server's own clauses.

**Why it beats the form.** A server can carry `(tick …)`, `(sched …)`,
`(queue …)` AND `(pipeline …)` — one server, four clauses, one state, one pane
row, no second form to keep in sync. That composability is the reason the clause
vocabulary exists at all.
