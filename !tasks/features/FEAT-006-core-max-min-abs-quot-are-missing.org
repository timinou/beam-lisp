* Why they matter beyond convenience

=max=/=min= are variadic reductions in Clojure and appear constantly in
aggregate code. PLAN-033 wave 4 implements datalog aggregates
(=count=/=sum=/=max=/=min=/=avg=); implementing =(max …)= over query results while
=max= itself does not exist in the language would be absurd.

=quot= is the truncating-division companion to the existing =mod= and =rem=;
having two of the three is an inconsistency a reader will trip over.

* Proposal

All four in =priv/core.bl= (they need no substrate):

#+begin_src clojure
(defn max
  "The greatest of its arguments. Variadic, as in Clojure."
  ([x] x)
  ([x y] (if (> x y) x y))
  ([x y & more] (reduce max (max x y) more)))

(defn min
  ([x] x)
  ([x y] (if (< x y) x y))
  ([x y & more] (reduce min (min x y) more)))

(defn abs
  "Absolute value. Preserves int/float type, as in Clojure 1.11+."
  [x]
  (if (< x 0) (- x) x))

(defn quot
  "Truncating integer division — the companion to the existing `mod`/`rem`."
  [n d]
  (erlang/div n d))
#+end_src

* Acceptance

- =(max 1 2 3)= = 3, =(min 1 2 3)= = 1, single-arity returns its argument
- =(max 1.5 2)= = 2 and =(max 2 1.5)= = 2 — mixed int/float compares numerically
- =(abs -3)= = 3, =(abs -3.5)= = 3.5, =(abs 3)= = 3
- =(quot 7 2)= = 3, =(quot -7 2)= = -3 (truncates toward zero, unlike =mod=)
- =(quot 7 0)= raises rather than returning a bogus value
- covered in =test/bl/core_additions_test.bl=, which exists for exactly this
  class of fn and documents why contract tests matter for them
