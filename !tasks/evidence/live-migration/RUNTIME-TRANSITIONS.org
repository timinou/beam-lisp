* Purpose and ownership
=proc.queue-runtime= is a shared native OTP scaffold around the existing
=proc.queue= engine. A queue's immutable generation becomes a retained handler,
not a replacement PID or a second scheduler. =proc.transition= coordinates
replayable queue and managed =proc.evolve= owner changes. Foundry owns sealed
callback selection, durable step intent, journal sequencing and serving slots.

* Native contracts
Prepare a queue descriptor under its immutable generation execution context:
#+begin_src lisp
(runtime/prepare :imports
 {:declaration {:of :document/import :protocol 2 :concurrency 4}
  :perform import-job
  :contract {:accept? valid-import?}})
#+end_src
This captures the exact environment and full physical generation graph. Native
leases retain direct callback roots and wrapper dependencies. Workers separately
retain the selected graph until completion, including when the queue owner dies.

=runtime/start!= starts the shared scaffold. Default callback/config publication
is FIFO in the queue owner; old workers retain their original retry/lease config,
callback and environment while new jobs run under the new handler. Original
producer metadata stays immutable. Old handlers remain retained until both
active attempts and durable required-generation references are zero. Retirement
then persists the producer fence, rechecks inventory and releases registrations.
A missing handler does not override nonzero durable obligations.

Uniqueness keys, partitions and entity identity cannot change through this
operation; those need an explicit data migration. Clock changes and payload
protocol regressions refuse. Existing compositional app-generated servers do not
silently become managed runtime owners: native transition authority is required.
Automatically lowering pure queue defservers onto this scaffold is a future
compatibility improvement; explicit native lifecycle integration works today.

* Replay and adoption
=transition/prepare!= runs under the sealed candidate context and receives:
#+begin_src lisp
{:previous-generation previous-digest
 :queues {:imports {:declaration {...} :perform import-job
                    :adoption {:digest migration-digest :protocol 2
                               :upcast upgrade-payload :accept? valid-import?}}}
 :owners {:index {:pid managed-owner :expected 1 :spec candidate-spec
                  :queues [:imports]}}}
#+end_src
The expected previous generation comes from the durable activation journal;
it is never inferred from mutable current state on replay. Queue adoption is
optional. Without adoption, old work executes its exact retained handler. With
adoption, =transition/adopt!= converts at most its explicit batch limit, preserves
identity/provenance, refuses pending provider effects and leaves failed jobs
failed. No deployment guesses provider outcomes or automatically retries failures.

=transition/activate!= publishes each native owner idempotently, then Foundry
publishes the serving router. This provides compatible old requests throughout;
it does not claim a multi-actor atomic commit. Partial batches replay safely.
=transition/retire!= uses native queue and managed message proofs.
=transition/close!= releases preparation leases; published owners independently
retain callbacks. Rebinding an already registered owner queue is idempotent;
adding new dependencies after retirement remains refused.

* Crash restoration
Before the selected application initializer, Foundry prepares old and candidate
queue descriptors under their respective sealed contexts and stages:
#+begin_src lisp
(runtime/restore! :imports candidate-digest [old-handler candidate-handler])
#+end_src
The initializer calls the usual =runtime/start!= with its candidate descriptor.
Startup chooses the persisted declaration as primary, reconstructs retained
handlers required by accepted jobs and rejects any missing generation. Candidate
activation can promote an already restored target. This handles crashes before
or after one queue publication without silently overwriting the persisted
version. The owner binds the selected environment; each worker binds its own
handler environment. Unconsumed staging is released by
=runtime/release-restoration!= in the lifecycle cleanup path.
